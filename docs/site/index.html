01 · The research question

Can video evidence identify when a rally is actually underway?

Long recordings contain pauses, preparation, and dead-ball time that can make useful highlights harder to review. This project studies how visual evidence can help locate meaningful Rally intervals while avoiding activity from neighboring courts.

A fixed camera gives stable views, but low angles, player overlap, a tiny fast shuttle, and adjacent courts make any single cue unreliable. The research combines Court geometry, anonymous player and Pose evidence, TrackNet shuttle candidates, and temporal context. It then studies LIVE, DEAD, UNKNOWN, and candidate START/END boundaries against editorial timelines.

Court ownership

Nearby courts can contain motion and shuttle detections that look relevant in image space.

Intermittent evidence

Pose and shuttle observations can be missing or uncertain from one frame to the next.

Editorial boundaries

What an editor keeps is not identical to physical shuttle-in-play or Hit truth.

What the evidence supports today

  • Implemented production: EventFirst; still PRODUCTION_NOT_READY.
  • Offline research: four same-date videos with measured state and interval comparisons.
  • Formal production output: no accepted Rally, KeepTimeline, or new export in the showcased study.

The gallery keeps these categories separate. A successful checkpoint or offline score is not a full end-to-end release result.
